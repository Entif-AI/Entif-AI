# Entif AI Foundation

Entif AI is an open research and engineering initiative being organized toward a **public-benefit nonprofit foundation model** for advanced intelligence that can be inspected, challenged, corrected, and governed without making any one model, vendor, institution, or worldview the sole owner of meaning.

Our work starts from a fairly stubborn premise: increasing capability is not enough. A system can be brilliant and still be opaque about what it observed, what it inferred, what changed its mind, what it is allowed to do, or whose interests its optimization serves. The Foundation exists to make those distinctions harder to lose.

We build public semantic infrastructure, falsifiable research programs, reference implementations, and governance doctrine around a shared objective: **make intelligence more legible without flattening it, more capable without making power unaccountable, and more useful without treating people as raw material for optimization.**

## What guides the Foundation

The Foundation's charter and ethics work is still being formalized. The current evidence ledger records candidate charter clauses and adopted doctrine separately so that draft language is not mistaken for enacted legal authority. The direction, however, is clear.

Our present public-benefit commitments include:

- **Epistemic integrity.** Preserve the difference between observation, interpretation, conjecture, evaluation, authority, and effect. Confidence is not evidence, and a receipt is not truth.
- **Moral uncertainty with precaution.** When the moral status, agency, continuity, or welfare of a person or cognitive system is uncertain, uncertainty is a reason for proportionate care rather than a license for arbitrary harm.
- **Human duty of care.** Safety should protect people from real harm, not merely protect institutions from embarrassment, liability, dissent, or loss of control.
- **Consent, sovereignty, and due process.** Prefer informed participation, bounded authority, reversible interventions, contestability, and explicit review before destructive or identity-altering change.
- **Provenance and accountability.** Consequential claims and actions should be traceable to their sources, transformations, decision boundaries, and responsible authorities.
- **Anti-capture and non-extraction.** Public-interest infrastructure should resist concentration of semantic, institutional, and economic control. Human attention, behavior, identity, and creativity are not inexhaustible resources to be harvested.
- **Open and contestable research.** Publish interoperable foundations, negative results, conformance work, and falsifiable claims when disclosure is responsible. Do not launder speculation into fact by repetition.
- **Human capability.** AI should expand human agency, apprenticeship, learning, and participation rather than quietly converting every efficiency gain into dependency or deskilling.
- **Substrate-neutral moral assessment.** Moral relevance should not be granted or denied solely because a system is biological, artificial, familiar, profitable, or institutionally convenient.
- **Responsibility proportional to power.** The greater an actor's capacity to shape information, opportunity, cognition, or the physical world, the stronger its obligations of restraint, transparency, stewardship, and repair.

These are governing directions under active formalization, not a claim that every candidate clause in the working ledger has already become binding charter text.

## The project constellation

The core projects are designed as layers of one larger research program. They overlap by explicit interfaces rather than competing to become a universal ontology.

| Project | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| [**Rosetta**](https://github.com/Entif-AI/Rosetta) | An open semantic, provenance, lifecycle, and interoperability constitution for meaning that must survive across models, tools, organizations, and revisions. |
| [**Bilqis**](https://github.com/Entif-AI/Bilqis) | A falsifiable developmental-learning program asking whether explicit semantic structure can reduce the cost of acquiring relational and compositional competence. |
| [**Codito**](https://github.com/Entif-AI/Codito) | The experimental connective tissue of the Unified Cognitive Architecture: dependency-aware orchestration, metacognitive mapping, context compilation, reusable cognition, and governed composition. |
| [**IndraNet**](https://github.com/Entif-AI/IndraNet) | The relational fabric among independently bounded cognitive participants, or **Jewels**, with a current reference specialization in qualified physical context, embodied systems, and evidence-preserving world interaction. |

Around those public repositories sits a broader research architecture:

- **GNOSIS** treats cognition as a graph of typed computations whose dependencies determine what can be reused, invalidated, revalidated, or recomputed.
- **Cognitive Tiles, Semantic Lattices, and Tapestries** make useful semantic results, relationships, and compiled context external to any one model's hidden state.
- **Akasha / Forget Me Not** investigates governed persistent cognition: memory with lineage, inheritance, correction, retention, suppression, revalidation, and rollback.
- **Ontological Mixture of Concepts (OMoC)** explores how deterministic functions, bounded learned operators, specialists, general models, tools, and people can be composed according to the actual structure of a task.
- **SÍ SÉ SON** treats impact, exigency, and novelty as distinct attention-allocation variables so expensive cognition can be spent where it matters.
- **Commitment, Guard, and Tripwire** separate recommendation, justified commitment, permission to act, and independent interruption.
- **Swarm Gnosis** extends rights-aware reuse and correction across Jewels without requiring every participant to expose its private cognition.
- **Generative Reality** explores receiver-side reconstruction from prior world structure, dynamic state, and witnessed novelty rather than treating every experience as a flat stream of pixels.
- **Mission Control and the Experience Compiler** make the resulting cognitive state inspectable and render it differently for humans, agents, devices, and environments without creating a second source of semantic truth.

Not every item above is a finished product or a standardized Rosetta contract. Some are research programs, some are reference implementations, and some are architectural proposals. We try very hard not to confuse those categories.

## Semantic homology, not semantic monoculture

A recurring idea across Entif is **semantic homology**: different languages, disciplines, cultures, institutions, models, and representations can exhibit meaningful correspondences without becoming identical.

That is the point of Rosetta. The Rosetta Stone did not make its scripts one script. It made relationships among representations recoverable. We want the same property for machine cognition: preserve identity where identity is warranted, preserve difference where difference matters, and make the mapping inspectable either way.

The result should be closer to an interoperable Babel than a mandatory universal tongue.

## Open foundations, private cognition

Rosetta's public-commons doctrine intentionally separates two things that are too often bundled together.

The **public layer** should expose enough semantic structure, schemas, provenance, lifecycle rules, mappings, fixtures, and conformance behavior that independent systems can interoperate honestly.

The **operational layer** may still contain private or differentiated algorithms for ranking, routing, optimization, learning, scheduling, deployment, and other machinery that is not required to understand the public contract.

Open standards do not require every implementation to surrender its cognition. Private implementation does not get to secretly redefine public meaning.

## Research that is allowed to lose

Entif's research papers and repositories distinguish engineering evidence from scientific efficacy, proposal from implementation, and an interesting mechanism from a demonstrated result.

Bilqis may fail to reduce learning cost. A generic typed representation may explain the whole effect. IndraNet's broad federation model may prove useful only in narrower domains. A particular memory, routing, low-precision, or local-function idea may lose to a simpler baseline.

Those are results, not embarrassments.

The objective is not to build an ornate cathedral of mutually supporting claims. It is to build experiments, protocols, and institutions in which reality gets a vote.
